Once your documents are added, just ask your agent in plain language. You don't need to name tools or
sections. DocuSense gives your agent the right moves, and it picks the cheapest one for your question.

## How your agent finds answers

Like a person skimming a long document:

1. **Looks at the table of contents** to see what's where.
2. **Searches** by meaning and by exact words, so "how long do we keep retrying?" finds a section
   titled "Retry Interval".
3. **Opens only the section that matters**, not the 40 pages around it.
4. **Quotes the passage** with a citation you can check.

## Kinds of questions it's good at

| You ask | What it does |
|---|---|
| "What does the handbook say about remote work?" | Finds and reads the relevant section |
| "What's the maximum file size we accept?" | Finds the exact rule and number |
| "List every requirement about passwords." | Collects every rule on that topic, across documents |
| "What does 'eligible employee' mean here?" | Finds where the term is defined |
| "What does Section 4.2 of the contract say?" | Opens that section directly |
| "Section 3 refers to 'Appendix B'. What's in it?" | Follows the reference |

## Rules and requirements

DocuSense recognises statements that set rules: **must**, **must not**, **should**, **may**, and limits
with numbers like "within 30 days" or "up to 1,000 characters". That means you can ask things like:

> What must contractors do before accessing customer data?

> Which rules mention a time limit?

and get the precise sentences back, rather than whole pages.

## Tips for better answers

- **Ask for the source.** "Quote the exact wording" or "cite the section" makes the agent include
  citations you can check.
- **Name the document** if you have several similar ones: "in the 2026 handbook…".
- **Ask about changes directly.** "What changed in the policy?" is much cheaper than asking the agent to
  read both versions.
- **Use the built-in prompts.** In apps that support them, pick **Answer with verified citations**,
  **What changed between versions** or **Audit for contradictions**.

## Search from the terminal

You can search your library yourself too:

```bash
docusense search "remote work allowance"
docusense define "eligible employee"
```
